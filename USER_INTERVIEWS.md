# User Interviews

## Interview 1 — R.S., CTO, 15-person SaaS startup

**Date:** Week of May 5, 2026 | **Duration:** 12 minutes | **Channel:** LinkedIn DM

**Background:** Technical co-founder managing a dev team of 8. Paying for Cursor Business, GitHub Copilot Business, and Claude Team.

**Quotes:**
- "I approved all three because different people asked for them at different times. I have no idea if there's overlap."
- "The finance team sent me a Slack last month asking me to justify the AI line item. I had nothing to say."
- "I assumed someone else was tracking whether we actually needed the team plans vs individual ones."

**Most surprising thing:** He didn't know Cursor Business and GitHub Copilot served near-identical use cases for most of his team. He assumed they were complementary.

**What it changed:** Added redundancy detection as a first-class feature — not buried in a footnote. The overlap between coding tools is the biggest savings opportunity and most users are unaware of it.

## Interview 2 — P.M., Head of Finance, 40-person startup

**Date:** Week of May 5, 2026 | **Duration:** 15 minutes | **Channel:** Cold email

**Background:** Non-technical finance lead. Reviews software spend monthly. Has been asked to cut SaaS by 15%.

**Quotes:**
- "I see 'AI Tools — $3,200/month' and I have no idea what's in there or whether it's being used."
- "I don't know enough to push back on the engineering team. They say they need it, I approve it."
- "If there was a report I could show them that said 'you can save $800 by doing X', I could have that conversation."

**Most surprising thing:** She wants to share the audit result with engineers as a conversation-starter, not a mandate. The shareable URL is critical for this — it needs to look credible and read like a CFO memo, not a startup landing page.

**What it changed:** Made the result page look more like a financial report. Added the "current spend → optimized spend" line in the hero. Made the reasoning per-tool explicit and defensible.

## Interview 3 — A.K., Senior Engineer / IC, 8-person startup

**Date:** Week of May 6, 2026 | **Duration:** 10 minutes | **Channel:** Discord (Indie Hackers server)

**Background:** Individual contributor paying for Cursor Pro and Claude Pro out of pocket, expensing it.

**Quotes:**
- "I just pay for whatever I need and submit an expense. Nobody's auditing it."
- "I've been on Claude Max for two months and I'm pretty sure I hit the Pro limits like twice."
- "If I could show my manager a report that said I'm on the right plan, that would actually be useful."

**Most surprising thing:** The tool is useful even when it shows no savings — it validates that someone is spending correctly. The "you're optimized" path needs to feel positive and shareable, not like a failure state.

**What it changed:** Rewrote the optimal state copy from "No savings found" (negative) to "You're Optimized — your stack is well-matched" (positive). Added the "notify me" CTA for the optimal state so it's still a lead capture opportunity.
