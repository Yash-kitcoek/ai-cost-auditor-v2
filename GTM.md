# Go-To-Market

## Exact target user

**Engineering Manager at a Series A SaaS company, 8–25 person team, $2M–$10M ARR.**

Not "startups" — specifically: the person who approves the AI tool subscriptions but didn't choose them. They inherited a stack that grew organically (one engineer got Cursor, another got Copilot, someone expensed Claude Pro) and now they're in a budget review and someone is asking why the AI tools line item is $3,400/month. They're not sure if that's good or bad. They want a benchmark and a justification — or ammunition to cut it.

Secondary user: **solo founder / indie hacker** who pays for 3–5 AI tools out of pocket and has the dim sense they're probably paying for overlaps.

## First 100 users in 30 days, $0 budget

**Day 1–3: Hacker News "Show HN"**
Post "Show HN: I built a free AI spend auditor — enter your tools, get a breakdown of what you could save." Write it in first person, describe the problem precisely, link to the tool. If it hits the front page (realistic — this is a useful, non-commercial tool), 500–2,000 visitors in 24 hours. That's 50–150 audits completed based on typical conversion rates for HN traffic.

**Day 3–7: Reddit, targeted**
Post in r/ExperiencedDevs and r/SaaS — not "I built a thing," but genuinely helpful posts. "I was trying to figure out if my team's AI tool spend was normal, so I built this — curious what other people's audits look like." Include a screenshot of a real (anonymised) result. Link in the comments when people ask.

**Day 7–14: X/Twitter cold DMs**
Find 50 EMs and CTOs at Series A companies (look at "VP Engineering" or "Head of Eng" bios, 500–5000 followers). DM: "Hey — I built a free tool that audits AI tool spend for engineering teams. Given your role I thought you might find it useful or have feedback. No pitch, no signup." 20% open rate × 10% try it = ~10 users. Worth doing for the feedback alone.

**Day 14–21: Indie Hackers and Lenny's**
Write a genuine post in Indie Hackers: "What I learned auditing $50k/month in AI tool spend across 50 startups" (using aggregate data from early users). The insight is the content — the tool is the CTA. This kind of post gets 50–100 clicks if it's genuinely useful.

**Day 21–30: Blogger/newsletter embed**
Find 5 newsletters about developer tooling (Pragmatic Engineer, TLDR, ByteByteGo, etc.) and offer them a free embeddable widget version. Even one placement → 200–500 new users.

## The unfair distribution channel

Credex has an existing network of companies that have purchased discounted AI credits. These are companies that have already demonstrated they care about AI tool costs. A cold email from Credex — "we built a free tool to audit your AI spend, no strings attached" — has a warm lead quality no external company could replicate. Every Credex customer is a potential user and a potential referrer. That's 0 CAC distribution at a warm trust level.
